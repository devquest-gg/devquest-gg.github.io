The opportunity Three billion people a month play something built in Unity. We re the team changing how those things get made — building AI that generates assets, writes and repairs code, and turns an idea into something playable faster than has ever been possible.

Our group owns the backend and operations behind all of it: the cloud infrastructure our AI products run on, the data and experimentation platforms that tell us what works, the evaluation systems that tell us whether the AI is actually any good, and the operations that keep everything running across regions and customers.

We re looking for a Staff Software Engineer who is genuinely AI-native — for whom agents and AI tooling are how the work gets done rather than a productivity add-on — and who sets direction rather than waiting for it. We ve built the foundations over the past few months — CI/CD, infrastructure-as-code, deployment standards — and they work. The platform is stable, and we intend to keep it that way. What we have not solved is how much of understanding it still depends on people: when a metric moves, someone works out what it means; when something degrades, a human reads dashboards and makes the call; when a model changes, we find out more slowly than we should.

None of that scales with the speed we re now shipping at, and none of it needs to stay manual.

What makes this a Staff role rather than a senior one is that several of these problems do not yet have a defined shape, and we are asking you to give them one.

We have more open directions than we can pursue. Deciding which ones are worth the team s time, convincing people you are right, and then building the thing — that is the job.

What you ll be doing Own how this team builds software with AI.

We use AI across our workflow today and we re not satisfied with how far it goes. You ll set the standard — agentic code review and repair, generated tests that stay honest as requirements change, the tooling and patterns the team adopts because they make the work faster. Not just working this way yourself, but moving the floor for everyone, and having the judgment to tell a tool that earns its place from one that doesn t.

Define what we measure, and rebuild observability so it explains itself.

Our telemetry has gaps, and much of what we collect is ambiguous, unlabelled, or only meaningful to whoever built it. You ll own the model of system health and business outcomes across our platform, make each metric self-explanatory, and replace manual review and manual reaction with automated interpretation.

Make diagnosis agentic, and decide how far that should go.

Build the troubleshooting layer that correlates signals, proposes root causes, and takes safe automated action — so that understanding a production issue doesn t start with a person reading graphs. Where the automation stops and a human stays in the loop is a judgment call with real blast radius, and it s yours to make.

Own the infrastructure side of evaluation, as the technical counterpart to our Quality Engineering Lead.

Our products generate code, find and fix bugs, and produce art and design — output with no single correct answer. We have the beginnings of an evaluation system and it isn t mature yet. You ll make evaluation fast, repeatable and cheap enough to run continuously, working as the engineering peer of the lead who owns the methodology.

Decide what AI does for gaming LiveOps.

Live games are managed continuously after launch: events and content, player behaviour and retention analysis, monetization, live health. We think Unity s data puts us in an unusual position to make that faster, smarter and far less manual.

We are early here and genuinely do not know yet what it becomes.

We re not asking you to execute a plan — we re asking you to form one, defend it, and know when to abandon it.

Make agentic systems affordable.

As more of our product and our own workflow runs on inference, the economics become an engineering problem — token efficiency, routing work to the cheapest model that can do it, managing what an agent has to keep in context, and recovering agent state so a failed run resumes instead of starting over. You ll own how we measure and optimize all of it, and set the budgets the rest of us build within.

Set technical direction for Unity AI s backend platforms.

Make the architectural calls that are expensive to reverse, run technical spikes with developers on Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud, and partner with product, design and other engineering teams to shape the roadmap rather than receive it.

Raise the engineers around you.

Design review, code review, mentoring, and the kind of technical argument that leaves the other person better at their job. We measure Staff impact partly by what the people near you are able to do a year from now.

What we re looking for Hands-on, current use of AI and agent tooling in your own engineering work.

Genuine daily practice, not familiarity with the concepts — and ideally experience pointing agents at problems beyond code generation. Expect to talk about what you have actually built A track record of setting technical direction that other people followed — choosing what to build and what not to, making the case, and owning the outcome when it was expensive to be wrong A track record of working on problems that weren t defined when you started — framing them, moving quickly to something real, and knowing when to change course Experience building and shipping complex, user-facing software products at scale Depth in observability — monitoring, logging, alerting and debugging with tools such as Grafana — and a point of view on making telemetry interpretable rather than merely present An automation instinct in operational work — you have replaced a manual judgment step in production with a system that makes it for you, or cut the human effort in a detection-to-resolution loop Hands-on experience with Microsoft Azure or Google Cloud Platform Backend service development, including API design, and comfort working in an established CI/CD and infrastructure-as-code environment Evidence of multiplying other engineers — mentoring, design review, or standards that outlived your involvement Ability to translate business and user needs into technical solutions Strong communication and interpersonal skills, with a proven ability to align stakeholders across disciplines and organizations We would rather hire someone exceptional in a few of these areas than adequate across all of them.
