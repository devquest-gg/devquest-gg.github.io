How credit evidence works

We don't verify credits.
We show the evidence.

Studios decide who gets credited, and they get it wrong all the time: people cut after a layoff, buried in "Special Thanks," or left off entirely. We don't claim to be the source of truth in their place. What we do is show who's willing to stand behind each credit, and who they are, so you can weigh it the way you'd judge a résumé.

What about published credits

This doesn't replace published credits

Published credits, like a game's own credit roll or the transcriptions on a site like MobyGames, are the backbone of the record, and they're often more complete than anything a young platform like ours has yet. We're not trying to replace them.

What they can't do is fill their own gaps. People get left off for reasons that have nothing to do with their work: layoffs, contractor policies, credit-cutoff dates, or plain mistakes, and a record built only from published credit rolls inherits every one of those gaps. DevQuest adds the layer they're missing: the people who were actually there, confirming each other's work. When your name is absent or wrong in the published record, this is where you can claim it and have your teammates back you up.

Why this works

Confirmers are real, and named

A confirmation isn't an anonymous like. Every confirmer appears on this same public credits graph, with their own work history you can open and inspect. You're never asked to trust a number, you can look at the people behind it.

Nothing is hidden

There's no single checkmark to game. A weak credit visibly reads as weak, a real one visibly earns strong backing. The difference is on the page, not locked in a score.

Confirming isn't free

Only someone who also shipped the game can confirm you, and their name is attached to it, staked on their own reputation. That's a real reference, not a vote.

Report anything

See a credit that's wrong? Flag it. It's marked "under review" until a person looks, and nothing is ever silently removed.

No system that relies on people can be made impossible to game, and we don't pretend otherwise. What we can do is make gaming visible: a fake can only ever reach the thin, unconfirmed end of the ladder, while real work earns names you can check. You weigh it in a glance, the same way you'd weigh a résumé.
