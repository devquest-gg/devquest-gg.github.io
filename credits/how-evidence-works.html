How credits earn trust

We don't verify credits.
We show the evidence.

Studios decide official credits, and they get them wrong all the time: people cut after a layoff, buried in "Special Thanks," or left off entirely. So we don't claim to have the one true answer. We show who's willing to stand behind each credit, and who they are, and let you judge it the way you'd judge a résumé.

Credits backed by peers, not just publishers.

Why this works

The industry is small

A fake "Lead Designer, God of War" isn't fooling an algorithm, it's trying to fool the hundreds of people who were actually there. Fraud on real games gets caught socially, fast.

Nothing is hidden

There's no single checkmark to game. A weak credit visibly reads as weak, a real one visibly earns strong backing. The difference is on the page, not locked in a score.

Confirming means something

Only someone who also shipped the game can confirm you, and their name is attached to it. That's a real reference, not an anonymous thumbs-up.

Report anything

See a credit that's wrong? Flag it. It's marked "under review" until a person looks, and nothing is ever silently removed.
