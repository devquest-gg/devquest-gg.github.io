How credit evidence works

We don't verify credits.
We show the evidence.

Studios decide official credits, and they get them wrong all the time: people cut after a layoff, buried in "Special Thanks," or left off entirely. So we don't claim to be the source of truth. We show who's willing to stand behind each credit, and who they are, and let you judge it the way you'd judge a résumé.

What about official credits

Why not just use MobyGames?

Official credits are valuable, and we show them too. But they aren't complete. Developers get left off for reasons that have nothing to do with their work: layoffs, contractor policies, studio rules, credit-cutoff dates, or plain mistakes. A centralized database can only ever be as good as those official lists, and inherits every gap in them.

DevQuest records both: the official credits, and the contributions the people who were there confirm. So your work doesn't disappear when an official system leaves it out. That's also why we don't treat "matches the official credits" as a higher truth than "confirmed by your teammates." When Riot, Ubisoft, or Insomniac leaves someone off, the peer record is the one that's right.

Why this works

Confirmers are real, and named

A confirmation isn't an anonymous like. Every confirmer appears on this same public credits graph, with their own work history you can open and inspect. You're never asked to trust a number, you can look at the people behind it.

Nothing is hidden

There's no single checkmark to game. A weak credit visibly reads as weak, a real one visibly earns strong backing. The difference is on the page, not locked in a score.

Confirming isn't free

Only someone who also shipped the game can confirm you, and their name is attached to it, staked on their own reputation. That's a real reference, not a vote.

Report anything

See a credit that's wrong? Flag it. It's marked "under review" until a person looks, and nothing is ever silently removed.

No system that relies on people can be made impossible to game, and we don't pretend otherwise. What we can do is make gaming visible: a fake can only ever reach the thin, unconfirmed end of the ladder, while real work earns names you can check. You weigh it in a glance, the same way you'd weigh a résumé.
